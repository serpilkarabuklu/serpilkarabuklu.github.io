## About me

**How does language organize meaning when multiple channels can express information simultaneously?**

This question drives my research in **sign language linguistics**, where I investigate how linguistic information is distributed, coordinated, and interpreted across the hands, face, body, space, and time. Sign languages offer a distinctive window into human language because multiple articulators can express different yet interacting linguistic structures simultaneously.

I am a Visiting Scientist at [Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago (TTIC)](https://ttic.edu/faculty/). My work examines **simultaneity, semantics, pragmatics, prosody, and information structure** in American Sign Language (ASL), Turkish Sign Language (TİD), and Nicaraguan Sign Language (NSL). I combine linguistic theory, experimental methods, quantitative movement analysis, and computational modeling to investigate how linguistic distinctions are encoded, how language experience shapes their production and interpretation, and how computational models represent them.

My broader research program, *Language Across Channels*, seeks to understand what these patterns reveal about **the architecture of human language and cognition**. By examining how linguistic information is organized across multiple channels, I aim to identify principles of linguistic structure and processing that extend beyond individual languages and modalities.

Explore my [research](/research/) and [publications](/publications/) to learn more.

## Research Background

I previously held postdoctoral research appointments at Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago (TTIC), working with the Speech and Language and Perception and Learning Systems groups, and at the University of Chicago's Sign Language Linguistics Laboratory. I earned my PhD in Linguistics from Purdue University.

My research brings together theoretical, experimental, and computational perspectives through interdisciplinary collaborations across sign linguistics, psycholinguistics, and artificial intelligence.

Outside research, I enjoy exploring new places, hiking, swimming, and spending time with my rescue dog, Oscar.

<img src="/images/article2.png" width="20" style="vertical-align:middle; margin-right:5px;"> **July 2026 | New Preprint – Head Movements Across Sign Languages** 
Our [new preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/jgq9n_v2) examines how ASL and TİD signers use head nod trajectories to express semantic and prosodic functions. Using kinematic analysis and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), we found crosslinguistic differences in prosodic head nods but similarities in semantic head nods. These findings provide new insights into how linguistic functions shape the temporal organization of movements across sign languages.
